My Ph.D. research in cartography examines how the context of data production can strengthen responsible and critical mapmaking. Through workshops with data visualization and mapmaking practitioners, I explore strategies and prototypes for embedding data provenance and context context into visualization workflows.

How to Map with Context

EuroCarto Conference 2026

Link to conference

Just as every map is based on the mapmaker’s conscious and unconscious decisions, every dataset – the basis of any map – is shaped by its production context. So, what can it look like to research and reflect on the dataset production context as part of mapmaking? By focusing on participant perspectives, this workshop at the European Cartographic Conference 2026 fosters the exchange and development of ideas needed to strengthen responsible data practices and integrate production context into the mapmaking workflow.

Topic and design of the workshops

Throughout the workshops, we will explore key questions together: How do we research dataset backgrounds? What information is required for a comprehensive fitness-for-use analysis? How can we adapt our designs to data caveats? And how can we move beyond being “strangers in the dataset” (D'Ignazio & Klein, p. 130) and “bridge the gap between data and reality” (Lin et al., p. 1189)?

The workshop will use creative, participatory methods (e.g. brainstorming, sketching, wireframing, and prototyping) to ensure inclusive engagement. These activities are inspired by the four key steps in co-creation:

  • Engage and exchange: Share challenges and experiences with data caveats, and how to address them in analysis and visualization.
  • Understand: Discussing user needs for a tool that supports researching and reflecting on dataset production context in visualization workflows.
  • Ideation and brainstorming: Gather and cluster relevant information regarding dataset production contexts (general or use-case specific), explore useful formats, and sketch simple prototypes.
  • Discuss and validate: Discuss (and optionally test) prototypes with other groups to refine ideas.

Centering practitioner perspectives, these workshops provide a space to brainstorm and prototype tools, guidelines, and strategies. Together, we will explore how to navigate data positionality and the decisions behind its creation, ultimately co-creating workflows for more responsible data practices.

Thematic Background

Data is never neutral or objective (D'Ignazio & Klein, Loukissas, Dalton & Thatcher). It is shaped by the production of data and “this context is essential for conducting accurate, ethical analysis” (D'Ignazio & Klein, p. 149). While various frameworks for documenting data production (e.g., metadata, data biographies, and datasheets for datasets) exist, responsible and reflexive data practices too often focus on data analysis processes and visualization decision-making and ethics. To use data responsibly, practitioners must acknowledge the entirety of the visualization process beginning with data production. In other words, acknowledging data’s positionality including their situatedness and contingency (Kitchin) must be paired with concrete strategies to interrogate data production practices, and innovative tools that make visible the production of data in analysis and visualization workflows.

In my work, I define responsible data practices as an intentional way of working with data that

  • takes into account the fact that data is never neutral or objective, and therefore considers the context in which the data was produced.
  • acts with responsibility for affected communities and considers possible (negative) consequences, aiming for an accurate, fair and equitable representation of communities and topics.

Target group of the workshops

These workshops are designed for practitioners with experience in mapping or data visualization who wish to use data (more) critically, responsibly, and ethically.

  • Backgrounds: All backgrounds are welcome.
  • Focus: While we primarily focus on quantitative data for thematic mapping, discussions and learnings can be transferred to a wide range of visualization use cases.
